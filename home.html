About Me

Research at the intersection of policy, people, and place

Master of Arts graduate with hands-on experience conducting research and distilling complex data into accessible reports. Over three years assisting individuals experiencing homelessness or food insecurity through community-based volunteer roles.

Detail-oriented and fluent in English and French, I bring a proactive, problem-solving approach to everything I do. I'm motivated by advancing equitable, anti-colonial community development — translating dense legislative and policy frameworks into clear recommendations that serve real people.

My academic background in History and Cultural, Social, and Political Thought grounds my research in rigorous historical and theoretical analysis. My MA thesis synthesized over 2,000 historical newspaper articles to examine race, gender, and American imperial expansion at the turn of the twentieth century.

Beyond academia, I have spent years working directly with communities: coordinating large-scale service events for unhoused individuals, supporting seniors facing food insecurity, and producing policy research for the Alliance to End Homelessness in the Capital Region.

Published Work

Policy Report  ·  Alliance to End Homelessness in the Capital Region  ·  2025

Regional Overview of Permitted Sheltering

A 60-page multi-jurisdictional policy report examining the legal and regulatory frameworks governing temporary overnight sheltering in public spaces across 13 municipalities and 3 electoral areas of the Capital Regional District. Analyzes municipal bylaws, provincial statutes, and federal legislation, alongside relevant case law, to identify inconsistencies and opportunities for improvement in public administration.

MA Thesis  ·  University of Victoria  ·  2023

"Expansion is too Clean a Name for it": Black Perspectives on the Spanish-Cuban-American and Philippines-American Wars

Original qualitative research synthesizing more than 2,000 historical newspaper articles to examine competing political perspectives and public discourse among Black Americans on U.S. imperialism from 1898 to 1902. A racial and gendered analysis of Black media, drawing on intersectionality theory to examine how race, gender, and class shaped responses to American expansion. Completed with a concentration in Cultural, Social, and Political Thought (CSPT) at the University of Victoria.
