About Me

Policy Analyst & Researcher · Victoria, BC

Background

Research at the intersection of policy, people, and place

Master of Arts graduate with hands-on experience conducting research and distilling complex data into accessible reports. Over three years assisting individuals experiencing homelessness or food insecurity through community-based volunteer roles.

Detail-oriented and fluent in English and French, I bring a proactive, problem-solving approach to everything I do. I'm motivated by advancing equitable, anti-colonial community development — translating dense legislative and policy frameworks into clear recommendations that serve real people.

My academic background in History and Cultural, Social, and Political Thought grounds my research in rigorous historical and theoretical analysis. My MA thesis — "Expansion is too Clean a Name for it": Black Perspectives on the Spanish-Cuban-American and Philippines-American Wars — synthesized over 2,000 historical newspaper articles to examine race, gender, and American imperial expansion at the turn of the twentieth century.

Beyond academia, I have spent years working directly with communities: coordinating large-scale service events for unhoused individuals, supporting seniors facing food insecurity, and producing policy research for the Alliance to End Homelessness in the Capital Region.

What drives me

Values & Approach

Anti-colonial practice

Equity-centred research

I approach all research and policy work through an equity lens, centering the voices and experiences of those most affected by systemic inequities — including Indigenous, racialized, and otherwise marginalized communities.

Clarity

Making the complex accessible

Effective research means nothing if it can't be understood by the people who need it most. I translate dense legislative and policy frameworks into clear, actionable language that drives real decisions.

Community

Grounded in service

Over three years of direct community service — with unhoused individuals, seniors facing food insecurity, and youth — keeps my work grounded in lived reality rather than abstraction.
